# Audit: Step 3 — Your Number v1
# Auditor: finplan-auditor | Date: 2026-06-08

## Verdict
**Revise.** Step 3 displays the headline `requiredPortfolio` straight from the single engine and lays out an honest, traceable math chain — its credibility instincts are good. But two issues stop a ship:

1. **Pattern-1 component-recompute smell (`Step3_YourNumber.tsx:42-61`).** Step 3 recomputes the 11-slider monthly retirement spend locally (`monthlyNeedToday`, `useMemo`) AND derives its own readiness predicate `hasRetirementData = monthlyNeedToday > 0`, instead of reading `res.annualRetSpend / 12` and `res.planReady` from the engine. The engine added `planReady` (`financialPlan.ts:357`) precisely as "the single source of the is-the-plan-ready predicate, so Step 4 and Step 5 don't each recompute it." Step 4 (`:150`) and Step 5 (`:221`) both adopted it; **Step 3 is the lone screen still computing its own**, so the gate it shows can drift from the rest of the app if the engine's spend/readiness definition ever changes. Same-input/same-result today, so it is not yet a divergence bug — it is the exact "calculation inside a component" the lessons warn against, on the one screen that should never disagree about whether the plan exists.
2. **The withdrawal-rate bracket cliff is invisible (`Step3_YourNumber.tsx:54-59, 169, 251`).** A retirement duration of 34 years yields 4.0% and 35 years yields 3.5% (canonical §1 step function, confidence: **medium** on the edges). That one-year change swings the entire required portfolio by ~14% (need ÷ 0.035 vs ÷ 0.040). Step 3 owns the Retirement-Duration slider (`:372-381`) AND displays the resulting rate as Step 04 of the math chain, yet `withdrawalRateExplanation` never hints that nudging duration across a bracket edge moves the whole number. A user dragging the slider sees their Number jump with no explanation.

Neither is an engine error — the math is canonical-correct and the chain is genuinely well-built. Both are this-screen presentation/architecture defects.

**Composite: 6.5/10.**

## Lens 1 — Nervous First-Timer ("Would someone who's never planned understand and trust this?")
### Critical
- None. The screen is strong for a first-timer: a single big green Number, a plain-English "How we calculated this" chain where "every number is traceable," and an empty state that tells the user exactly what to do ("Complete Steps 1 and 2 first").
### Improvement
- **The bracket cliff can spook a first-timer with no explanation (convergent with Lens 2).** Dragging Retirement Duration from 34→35 silently drops the rate 4.0%→3.5% and pushes the Number up ~14%. To a nervous user this looks like the tool glitched. A one-line hint near the duration slider or the WR chain row ("longer retirements use a more cautious withdrawal rate") would convert a scary jump into a teaching moment. `[NEEDS-LIVE]` to see the magnitude on screen.
- **"× multiplier" jargon is unglossed (`:251`).** Step 04 reads "÷ 4.0% withdrawal rate = 25× multiplier." A first-timer may not know the 25× and the 4% are the same fact (1/0.04). The `withdrawalRateExplanation` note helps, but does not connect "4%" to "25×." Worth a half-sentence.
- **The custom-WR slider has no guardrail copy on the consequence (`:414-432`).** A user can drag withdrawal rate to 6.0% and watch their Number shrink with only "Custom withdrawal rate (overrides auto-calculation)" — no note that a higher rate means a higher chance of running out (the honest counterweight). Educational gap, not an error.

## Lens 2 — Skeptical CFP ("Is the math correct, defensible, and cited?")
### Critical
- None. Every displayed figure traces to canonical and the single engine: hero = `res.requiredPortfolio` (§2), chain = `monthlyNeedToday` → `res.annualRetSpend` (§2 B) → `res.futureAnnualNeed` (§2) → `res.requiredPortfolio` (§2). WR cited as "Trinity Study" via `withdrawalRateExplanation` (`:56`). The row-9 COLA/pension caveats are present and correct at the inputs (see scan).
### Improvement
- **WR bracket attribution is correct but the edges are uncaveated (canonical §1, confidence: medium).** `withdrawalRateExplanation` labels ≥35→"Extra conservative," <15→"Short duration rate," but canonical §1 explicitly notes the ≥35→3.5% and <15→5.0% extensions are "the tool's own interpolation… CFP to validate or refine the bracket edges." The copy presents all four brackets with equal confidence. A CFP would want the interpolated edges flagged as the tool's heuristic, not Bengen's finding. Tooltip-level fix.
- **Hero `requiredPortfolio` is nominal year-1 with no today's-$ companion (row 15 adjacent).** Canonical §2's display convention dual-shows today's-$ + nominal for Projected; `requiredPortfolio` is defined as the unchanged year-1 snapshot, so it is defensible as-is, but the hero gives no denomination label at all ("total portfolio needed at retirement," `:153`). Stating "(in {retYear} dollars)" would remove ambiguity. Defensible, not wrong.

### Critical

### Improvement
- **Off-canonical palette drift, one instance a contrast fail (Pattern 3 / canonical §6-7).** Three hexes on this screen are absent from canonical §6 and `global.css`: `#cbd5e1` (math-chain row labels, `:318`), `#8b5cf6` (Step 02 chain-bubble color, `:236`), `#6ee7b7` (the "Your Retirement Number" badge text, `:117`).
  - `#cbd5e1` on the dark bg ≈ **10.5:1** — legible, **passes** AA/AAA; pure drift (should be the canonical `#94a3b8` or `#f8fafc`).
  - `#6ee7b7` (emerald-300) badge text ≈ passes (light tone); drift only.
  - `#8b5cf6` (violet-500) ≈ **3.83:1** on `#0f172a` — **fails AA for normal text** (passes large-text 3:1 only). On this screen it is used as a step-bubble accent color/border (`:298-307`, on an `18`/`40` alpha fill) where it reads as decoration, not body text, so the §7 *text* rule is borderline rather than clearly breached — but the hue itself is **not in the canonical palette at all** (§6 has no purple), same drift the Step-5 audit flagged. `[NEEDS-LIVE]` to confirm whether any violet renders as small text.
- **Math-chain intermediate values are not monospace (glossary rule).** The glossary mandates "All financial numbers render in monospace." Only the **result** value uses `fontFamily: 'monospace'` (`:345`); the three intermediate chain values (`:339-351`, the non-`isResult` branch) and the hero trio stats (`:182-205`) render in the sans stack with `tabular-nums` only. Numbers-in-monospace is a standing UI rule; the chain partially violates it. Polish-level, but it is a named rule.

## Lens 4 — Regression-Across-Screens ("Does the same input produce the same number everywhere, and did this break another view?")
### Critical
- **Step 3 is the lone screen recomputing the readiness predicate + retirement-spend sum the engine already owns (Pattern 1).** `monthlyNeedToday` (`:42-52`) re-adds the 11 sliders that `res.annualRetSpend` (`financialPlan.ts:267-269`) already sums, and `hasRetirementData = monthlyNeedToday > 0` (`:61`) re-derives what `res.planReady` (`financialPlan.ts:357`) already exposes. The engine comment states planReady exists "so Step 4 and Step 5 don't each recompute it"; **Step 4 (`:150`) and Step 5 (`:221`) read `res.planReady`, Step 3 does not.** Today the values agree (annualRetSpend = monthlyNeedToday×12), so there is **no live divergence** — but this is the precise Pattern-1 anti-pattern: a quantity owned by the engine, recomputed in a component, on the one screen whose entire job is to show "your number." If the engine's spend definition or readiness rule changes (e.g. a 12th category, or planReady gaining a guard), Step 3 silently drifts from Step 4/5. **Fix: `const monthlyNeedToday = res.annualRetSpend / 12;` and gate on `res.planReady`.** Severity Critical as a regression-surface, not as a current wrong number.
### Improvement
- **`multiplierLabel` recomputes `1 / withdrawalRate` (`:64`).** Minor: derives the 25× from `res.withdrawalRate`. Reads the store value so it cannot diverge from the displayed rate, but it is arithmetic the component could avoid if the engine exposed the multiplier. Low priority.
