# Auditor: finplan-auditor | Date: 2026-06-08

## Verdict
**Revise.** Step 1 reads its budget totals (`currentFixed`, `currentInvest`, `currentGuiltFree`, `totalAllocated`) straight from the single engine and recomputes no projection, so it keeps rows 2/8/11/13 clean. Its budget math is internally correct and it reconciles allocation to take-home honestly (the "Unallocated" badge + overspend warning are a genuine strength). Two issues block "ship":

1. **STOP-SHIP — the budget-allocation panel renders its category labels and percentages as `rgba(255,255,255,0.45)`, `rgba(255,255,255,0.30)`, and `rgba(255,255,255,0.20)` text, which compute to 4.20:1, 2.64:1, and 1.82:1 over the glass card — all below the 4.5:1 AA floor, two of them failing every threshold.** The "Fixed Costs / Investing / Guilt-Free Spending" labels and "% of income" subtitles (`Step1:226,234,260,268,294,302`, white@0.45 = 4.20:1) and *every* "Ramit's target…" helper + the Sethi attribution (`:239,273,307,333,344`, white@0.30 and @0.20 = 2.64:1 / 1.82:1) sit below the line. This is a fresh recurrence of errors.md row 5 (Pattern 4) using `rgba` white instead of the named banned hexes — same defect, different spelling, on the app's most input-dense screen. The regression protocol escalates a recurrence to Critical.
2. **STOP-SHIP (content/trust, Wave-3 focus) — 32 of 35 `MoneyInput`s have NO `helperText`.** Only take-home, 401(k), and employer-match explain themselves; the other 32 inputs (Rent, Property Tax, Other Insurance, Debt Minimums, Banking Fees, Personal Development, etc.) are bare labels. The product's whole premise (CLAUDE.md: "a coach, not a calculator," for "someone who has never done serious financial planning") fails at the first screen: a first-timer is asked to fill 35 fields with no "why," no "monthly vs annual," and no examples. This is precisely the content gap the task names as the Wave-3 focus, confirmed quantitatively.

A latent third issue: the take-home pay default is **$5,000** while all 35 spend/invest fields default to **$0**, so on first load the budget bar is empty and reads "$5,000 unallocated — consider increasing investments." That is an honest empty state (good), but it is also the only guidance a blank-slate user gets.

## Lens 1 — Nervous First-Timer ("Would someone who's never planned understand and trust this?")
### Critical
- **32 of 35 inputs have no helper text — the coaching is absent exactly where the user starts (Wave-3 focus, confirmed).** A first-timer opening Step 1 faces 35 dollar fields. Three explain themselves; 32 do not. Specific harms: (a) **monthly-vs-annual ambiguity** — "Property Tax," "Car Insurance," "Life Insurance," "Banking Fees" are commonly paid annually/semi-annually, but the card says "Monthly Budget" and there is no per-field reminder to divide; a user who types their $1,800 annual property-tax bill as a monthly cost silently corrupts every downstream number including the Step 5 "your number." (b) **no examples / no "why"** — "Personal Development," "Other Insurance," "Misc" are undefined; the user guesses what belongs where. (c) The two fields that DO have helpers (401k, employer match) show how good this can be ("NOT part of take-home pay, but IS part of investments") — the contrast with the 32 bare fields is stark. This is the single biggest gap between the screen and the product's "coach, not calculator" charter.
- **Low-contrast labels compound the above.** The three bucket labels users navigate by — "Fixed Costs," "Investing," "Guilt-Free Spending" (`:226,260,294`) — render at 4.20:1 (below AA), and the guidance under them ("Ramit's target: 50–60%", ":≥ 20%", "Your money, your choice") at 2.64:1, plus the Sethi attribution at 1.82:1. The orientation text and the only inline coaching present are the hardest things to read. *Convergent with Row 5 / Lens 3.*
### Improvement
- **Silent contribution seeding may surprise.** Entering investment amounts here silently sets `monthlyContrib` (via the subscriber), which later drives Step 4's projection. The user gets no on-screen indication that "Investments" on Step 1 becomes their "monthly contribution" on Step 4. Correct behavior (row 7), but a one-line helper ("This becomes your monthly investing contribution in Step 4") would close the loop. *Ties to Row 14 — the budget here is the hidden driver downstream.*
- **No "annual? we'll convert" affordance.** Given the monthly-vs-annual risk above, an explicit toggle or per-field "monthly" suffix would prevent the most damaging silent error a first-timer can make. Designer + content.
- **Collapsed-by-default depth.** All three columns and their inner sections are `<details>` (collapsed). A first-timer may not realize there are 14 fixed-cost fields hidden inside three nested accordions. `[NEEDS-LIVE]` to confirm default open/closed state and whether the scope of the task is discoverable.

## Lens 2 — Skeptical CFP ("Is the math correct, defensible, and cited?")
### Critical

The budget math is correct and the bucket definitions are sound. `currentInvest` includes employer match (for the investments *total*) while `currentInvestForBudget` excludes it from the allocation-vs-take-home reconciliation (`financialPlan.ts:319-323`) — this is the **correct** treatment (match is not take-home pay) and the UI states it ("including employer match - not part of take-home pay," `Step1:500`). Good rigor.
### Improvement
- **"Ramit's target: 50–60%" / "≥ 20%" are presented as hard targets without a source link.** They match Sethi's Conscious Spending Plan bands (Fixed 50–60%, Investments ≥10% commonly cited; the screen says ≥20%) and the screen attributes the framework ("Based on Ramit Sethi's Conscious Spending Plan"). The ≥20% investing target is defensible but is on the aggressive end of Sethi's stated 5–10% savings / 10% investing guidance — worth a content check that the exact percentages match the cited source. Low severity; attribution is present, the specific number should be verified. *Content + CFP.*
- **The allocation reconciliation is honest** (overspend warning at `:315`, unallocated nudge at `:331`) — this is exactly the "reconcile allocation to take-home" behavior the audit brief asks about, and Step 1 does it correctly. Noting as a strength, not a defect.
